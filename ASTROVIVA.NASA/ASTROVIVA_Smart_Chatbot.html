ASTROVIVA

Astronaut Health Monitor

Far from any hospital, the first sign of a problem is a small shift in a familiar number. ASTROVIVA compares every reading with that astronaut's own normal and tells the medical team what changed.

Enter mission control How it works
  • 6 vital indicators + ECG
  • Personal baseline per astronaut
  • English and Arabic

Why it matters

A long way from the nearest doctor.

On a deep-space mission the medical team on Earth sees data late, and the crew cannot walk into a clinic. Catching a slow drift early is the difference between a check-in and an emergency.

Earth to Mars signal delay

3–22MIN ONE WAY
  • A question to ground control can wait minutes for an answer. The crew needs a first read on their own health right now.
  • ASTROVIVA works locally and offline: analysis, alerts, the report and the assistant all run on the device.

Normal is different for every person

A resting heart rate of 74 is ordinary for one astronaut and a warning for another. Drag the reading and watch how ASTROVIVA judges it against this astronaut's own baseline (62 ± 4 bpm), not a population average.

From sensor to decision

Four steps, all visible to the medical team.

  1. STEP 1

    Collect

    Heart rate, SpO₂, respiration, temperature, sleep and activity, plus the ECG rhythm. Live from a stream, typed in, or imported from CSV.

  2. STEP 2

    Compare

    Each value is measured in standard deviations from the astronaut's personal baseline and checked against clinical reference limits.

  3. STEP 3

    Detect

    Single out-of-range values, slow drifts across three readings, and combined patterns such as stress or several indicators moving together.

  4. STEP 4

    Report

    Ranked alerts, trend charts against the baseline band, a copy-ready health report and a bilingual assistant that explains each number.

Health guidance library

        
Quick explanations to help the crew understand what to measure and why.

        

          
Blood pressureUse an approved cuff, follow the device instructions, sit or stabilize as directed, and record the time and position.

          
Blood glucoseUse the mission-approved meter and follow its sampling and hygiene instructions. Record whether the reading was fasting or after a meal.

          
Mood & fatigueA brief daily self-rating can help reveal changes over time. A rating is a check-in, not a mental-health diagnosis.

          
Repeat an unexpected readingCheck the device, technique and context, then repeat according to mission medical procedures. Do not delay urgent help for severe symptoms.

Evidence base

      
Built to be tested on real recordings.

      
Each open dataset below has a specific job in validating the thresholds and the rhythm checks. The demo runs on synthetic data modelled on typical ranges until real recordings are loaded.

    

    

      
ECG · 360 Hz
MIT-BIH Arrhythmia
Annotated two-channel ECG. Used to test the rhythm check: normal against irregular beats.

      
ECG · 12-lead
PTB-XL
Large labelled 12-lead ECG set. Used for ST-change and waveform morphology detection.

      
Sleep
Sleep-EDF
Polysomnography with sleep-stage labels. Used for sleep duration, quality and disruption patterns.

      
Vitals
MIMIC-IV
Hospital vitals: HR, SpO₂, RR, temperature. Used for realistic ranges and abnormal patterns. Needs credentialed access.

      
Literature
PubMed
Peer-reviewed papers. Used to justify reference ranges and alert limits, especially for spaceflight physiology.

      
Hub
PhysioNet
The open archive that hosts the signal datasets above: ECG, respiration, SpO₂.

    

    
Thresholds are illustrative and must be validated by medical professionals.
